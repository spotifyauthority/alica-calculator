What Is Alice Calculator and How Do You Use It?

A calculator that shows its work, not just the answer.

84 ÷ 7 7 into 8 → 1, remainder 1
bring down 4 → 14 ÷ 7 = 2
answer: 12

Stuck checking a long division problem and tired of calculators that only show the final number? Alice Calculator, also called Calculadora Alicia, was built for exactly that problem. Instead of just giving you an answer, it shows the full working, the same way you'd solve it by hand on paper.

Alice Calculator is a free calculator tool that solves addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, square roots, and factorization while displaying every step of the process. It's available as a website and as a mobile app, and it works without an internet connection once installed.

Why Alice Calculator Is Different

Most calculators give you one number and stop there. Alice Calculator shows the steps leading up to that number — carrying digits in addition, borrowing in subtraction, the full layout of a long division problem. That step-by-step view is the whole point of the tool, and it's what separates it from a basic phone calculator.

The idea reportedly started as a simple homework fix: a parent built the first version to help a child check division homework the way she was taught to do it on paper, rather than just getting a bare result with no explanation.

Who Alice Calculator Is Built For

  • Students checking homework and wanting to see where a mistake happened, not just that one happened
  • Teachers who want to show a worked example quickly
  • Anyone relearning basic math, since seeing the steps again can refresh skills faster than a plain answer

It's built for everyday arithmetic, not advanced calculus or graphing — if you need a scientific or graphing calculator for higher-level work, this isn't the right tool.

  • Free to use — no payment required for the core features

How to Use Alice Calculator

  1. Open the website or mobile app.
  2. Pick the operation you need — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, square root, or factorization.
  3. Enter your numbers.
  4. Review the result along with the full step-by-step breakdown.

That's the entire process. There's no setup, login, or learning curve involved.
